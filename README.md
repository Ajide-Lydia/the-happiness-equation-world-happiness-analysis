## 📌 Project Overview

What makes a country happy? Is it money? Freedom? Health? Or something deeper?

This project analyses the **World Happiness Report dataset across 5 years (2015–2019)**, covering countries worldwide and the key factors that contribute to their happiness scores. Rather than treating happiness as a single number, this analysis breaks it down into its core components GDP per capita, social support, healthy life expectancy, freedom to make life choices, generosity, and perceptions of corruption to understand what truly moves the needle on national wellbeing.

The core question: **has the world become happier, and what is actually driving the answer?**

## ⚙️ Analysis & Dashboard (Excel)

All visualisation and analysis was built entirely in Excel using:

- **Pivot Tables** to aggregate and summarise data across years and countries
- **Line charts** to track happiness score trends over time by country
- **Stacked bar charts** to break happiness down by its contributing components
- **Bar charts** to compare average happiness scores and GDP relationships across countries
- **Slicers** for interactive filtering by year and country
- **Dashboard layout** combining all visuals into one cohesive, titled report

## 📊 Dashboard Features

- **Happiness Scores by Country** — line chart tracking score trends for top-ranking countries from 2015 to 2019
- **Happiness Score by Year** — overall global average trend line showing whether the world got happier or sadder over 5 years
- **Country by GDP and Happiness Score** — dual-axis chart comparing economic wealth against happiness scores across nations
- **Happiness by Components** — stacked horizontal bar chart breaking each country's happiness score into its six pillars: social support, healthy life expectancy, freedom to make life choices, generosity, perceptions of corruption, and GDP per capita

## 🔍 Key Insights

1. **The world dipped in 2017 but is trending upward** — the Happiness Score by Year chart shows a decline between 2016 and 2017, followed by a recovery and the highest recorded average in 2019. Global wellbeing is resilient — even after setbacks, the overall trajectory is upward.

2. **Happiness is built on three core pillars** — the Happiness by Components chart consistently shows that **social support, economic wealth (GDP per capita), and healthy life expectancy** are the dominant contributors to a country's happiness score across all five years.

3. **High GDP is a foundation, not a guarantee** — the Country by GDP and Happiness Score chart reveals that several countries prove that strong social systems can produce top happiness rankings even without the highest GDP. Money gets a country started — social infrastructure keeps it happy.

4. **Finland dominated the rankings, followed by Norway, Denmark, and Switzerland** — these Nordic and Western European nations held the top positions consistently, with Finland claiming the number one spot for two consecutive years, driven not just by wealth but by exceptionally high social support and freedom scores.

5. **Data quality improved significantly over time** — the 2017–2019 datasets were notably more focused, consistent, and complete than the 2015–2016 files, reflecting the global improvement in standardised happiness measurement methodology over the five-year period.

6. **Geography and culture cluster happiness** — countries in the same region consistently score within similar ranges, suggesting that geography and shared cultural values play a significant role in national wellbeing beyond economic factors alone.

7. **Top-ranking countries maintain stable happiness levels** — despite year-on-year global fluctuations, the leading nations (Finland, Norway, Denmark, Switzerland, Iceland) showed remarkably consistent scores across all five years, proving that once a strong social foundation is built, happiness is sustainable.

---

## 💡 Recommendations

- **Prioritise social support systems over pure economic growth** — the data consistently shows that social infrastructure closes the gap between wealthy and moderately wealthy nations in happiness rankings
- **Nations experiencing a happiness decline should audit their social support networks first** — GDP alone cannot explain or fix drops in happiness scores
- **Global happiness measurement should be standardised earlier** — the inconsistency in the 2015–2016 raw data shows how fragmented reporting undermines longitudinal analysis
- **Regional learning should be encouraged** — given that geography clusters happiness, lower-ranked nations can study and adopt policies from their higher-ranked regional neighbours for the most culturally relevant lessons

---

## 📝 Conclusion

This 5-year analysis shows that money gets a country started, but **social support and health are what keep people happy**. Even when the world faces challenges as the 2017 dip shows the data reveals that we are resilient and trending toward a happier future. Happiness is not a luxury driven by wealth alone; it is a system built on how well a society takes care of its people.
